Solid Electrolyte Interphase (SEI) Cryogenic Transport Physics

Nanoscale mechanics, Arrhenius kinetics, and equivalent electrical circuit modeling for low-temperature lithium-ion cell degradation.

1. Interfacial Desolvation & Arrhenius Kinetics

During lunar cold conditions (down to $-100^\circ\text{C}$), the kinetic rate-limiting step transitions from bulk liquid-state diffusion to ion desolvation through the inorganic inner SEI layer ($\text{Li}_2\text{CO}_3$, $\text{LiF}$). The interfacial charge-transfer resistance $R_{\text{ct}}$ and SEI migration resistance $R_{\text{SEI}}$ scale exponentially with absolute temperature $T$:

$$R_{\text{SEI}}(T) = R_0 \cdot \exp\left( \frac{E_{a,\text{SEI}}}{k_B T} \right), \quad i_0 = n F k^0 C_{\text{Li}^+}^{(1-\alpha)} \exp\left( -\frac{\Delta G^\ddagger}{R T} \right)$$

Where $E_{a,\text{SEI}} \approx 0.65\text{--}0.85\,\text{eV}$ represents the activation barrier for $\text{Li}^+$ desolvation from ethylene carbonate shells. At $223\,\text{K}$ ($-50^\circ\text{C}$), $R_{\text{SEI}}$ inflates by over $60\times$, generating lethal localized overpotentials.

2. Mechanical Micro-Cracking & Plating Risk

Imposing high step currents on cold electrodes creates severe lithium chemical potential gradients. When the anode potential drops below $0\,\text{V}$ vs. $\text{Li}/\text{Li}^+$, metallic lithium deposits as high-surface-area dendrites. Thermal contraction creates mismatch shear stress $\sigma_{\text{int}}$:

$$\eta_{\text{anode}} = \Phi_{\text{solid}} - \Phi_{\text{electrolyte}} - U_{\text{eq}} = \eta_{\text{ohm}} + \eta_{\text{ct}} + I_{\text{load}} R_{\text{SEI}} < 0\,\text{V}$$
$$\sigma_{\text{eff}} = \frac{E_{\text{SEI}}}{1 - \nu_{\text{SEI}}} (\Delta \alpha \cdot \Delta T) + \gamma \cdot \frac{d\ln V}{dt} > \sigma_{\text{fracture}}$$

If $\eta_{\text{anode}} < 0$, destructive dendrite nucleation ruptures the brittle SEI film ($\sigma_{\text{fracture}} \approx 1.2\,\text{MPa}$), initiating catastrophic runaway or permanent capacity degradation during early lunar ascent.

3. Onboard Electrochemical Impedance Spectroscopy (EIS)

The BatterySEI embedded firmware injects multi-sine low-amplitude perturbation current $I(t) = \sum I_k \sin(2\pi f_k t + \theta_k)$ across intermediate frequencies ($0.5\,\text{Hz} \le f \le 5\,\text{kHz}$). The complex impedance is decomposed via real-time discrete Fourier transforms:

$$Z(\omega) = R_{\Omega} + \frac{R_{\text{SEI}}}{1 + (j \omega R_{\text{SEI}} C_{\text{SEI}})^{\alpha_{\text{SEI}}}} + \frac{R_{\text{ct}}}{1 + (j \omega R_{\text{ct}} C_{\text{dl}})^{\alpha_{\text{dl}}}} + Z_W(\omega)$$

The characteristic intermediate arc apex occurs at $\omega_{\text{peak}} = (R_{\text{SEI}} C_{\text{SEI}})^{-1}$. Tracking the apex coordinates $[\text{Re}(Z), -\text{Im}(Z)]$ reveals real-time SEI integrity without intrusive offline laboratory bench testing.

4. Micro-Fabrication & Internal Heating Bridge

The flight unit pairs space-grade radiation-hardened GaN-on-SiC bidirectional half-bridges with multi-layer nickel foil excitation traces embedded directly between jellyroll cell leaves. When $R_{\text{SEI}} > R_{\text{critical}}$, high-frequency AC excitation ($10\,\text{kHz}$) drives internal Joule heating:

$$\dot{Q}_{\text{internal}} = I_{\text{AC,rms}}^2 \cdot R_{\Omega}(T) + \frac{1}{2} C_{\text{SEI}} V_{\text{AC}}^2 \cdot \omega \tan(\delta)$$

Uniform volumetric heating heats the core at $12.4^\circ\text{C}/\text{min}$ from inside out without consuming external radioisotope heating units, preserving nominal cell lifetime across $200+$ lunar day-night cycles.
